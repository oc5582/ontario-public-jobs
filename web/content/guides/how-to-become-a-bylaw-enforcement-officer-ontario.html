To become a by-law enforcement officer in Ontario, you apply to a city or town that is hiring and meet the requirements in its job ad. Ontario law lets each municipality appoint its own officers. In the two City of Brampton ads in this guide, the steps after you apply can include tests or exams and an interview. The person who gets the job needs a criminal record search.

What a by-law enforcement officer does

A by-law is a local law made by a municipality, such as a city or town. A by-law enforcement officer is the person a municipality appoints to enforce those laws. By-laws cover things like parking, noise, long grass, garbage, signs, animals and business licences. Officers check that people follow by-laws. In the Brampton ads, that means investigating complaints, educating the public and, when needed, issuing notices, orders and tickets.

Ontario's Community Safety and Policing Act, 2019 sets out the role in section 55:

  • a municipality may appoint persons to enforce its by-laws, and
  • those officers are peace officers for the purpose of enforcing municipal by-laws.

The same Act defines "police officer" and says that term does not include a municipal by-law enforcement officer. So a by-law officer is a peace officer when enforcing by-laws, but is not a police officer.

Ontario's Provincial Offences Act also counts a by-law enforcement officer as a provincial offences officer while doing their duties. Both Brampton postings in this guide list testifying in court as part of the job.

The City of Toronto's Municipal Licensing and Standards division has several units that enforce by-laws or issue licences. Its page lists them:

Job titles vary. Ontario laws use both "municipal by-law enforcement officer" and "municipal law enforcement officer". The Brampton jobs below are called property standards and parking enforcement officers.

Who hires by-law officers in the GTA

In the GTA, the City of Toronto and the City of Brampton both have enforcement divisions. Other cities and towns appoint officers too. The MLEOA says it represents over 250 Ontario municipalities. On November 26, 2026, the City of Brampton had 23 open jobs on PublicJobs.ca, and all 23 showed pay. A search of PublicJobs.ca on November 27, 2026 found no other GTA municipal by-law officer posting.

The Toronto and Region Conservation Authority (TRCA) also has an enforcement team. On November 26, 2026, it had an opening for an Administrative Enforcement Technician in its Enforcement and Compliance unit. It is an office support job, not an officer job. Its ad lists $34.35 to $39.80 an hour (the January 1, 2027 rate) for a 12-month contract.

Two Brampton postings from November 2026: duties, pay and requirements

These two City of Brampton postings were open on PublicJobs.ca on November 26, 2026. Both sit in Brampton's Enforcement and Bylaw Services division and report to the Supervisor, By-law Enforcement.

Job Posted pay Closing date
Jr Property Standards Officer $35.44 an hour November 27, 2026
Parking and Sign Enforcement Officer $31.75 an hour Open until filled

Both jobs are part-time, 24 hours a week, and temporary for 6 months. The property standards ad has 1 position. The parking ad has 10 positions and lists shift work and variable hours.

Jr Property Standards Officer

The ad says this officer enforces by-laws and provincial law on the minimum upkeep of properties. That covers repairs, the inside of buildings and yards. The duties include:

  • answering complaints and investigating private properties,
  • spotting safety hazards on buildings and yards,
  • issuing Notices of Non-compliance, Orders to Comply and Property Standards Orders,
  • directing City contractors on "self-help" work, such as cutting long grass or removing garbage, and checking the work when it is done, and
  • testifying in court and doing community outreach.

The ad asks for:

  • Grade 12, plus a program of over 1 and up to 2 years in enforcement, building regulations or equivalent,
  • over 2 months and up to 6 months of related experience,
  • a valid Ontario non-probationary Class G licence with a clear driving record, and being able to get a City of Brampton driver's permit,
  • being able to work in all weather, and
  • Microsoft Office skills, teamwork, and strong writing, speaking and customer service skills.

Parking and Sign Enforcement Officer

This officer enforces parking by-laws for parked vehicles, investigates Traffic By-law violations, and removes illegal signs from boulevards and roads. The ad says officers issue Penalty Notices or Parking Infraction Notices, keep a daily journal of their work and evidence, and testify in court, hearings and tribunals. They patrol an assigned area in a City vehicle.

The ad asks for Grade 12 plus a program of over 1 and up to 2 years in Law and Security, Police Foundations or equivalent. It also asks for over 2 months and up to 6 months of related experience, a valid non-probationary Class G licence, and the ability to get a City of Brampton (COB) permit.

Both ads describe confrontational moments with the public. One says officers must stay tactful in "potentially volatile situations."

Training and certification

A program after Grade 12. The Brampton ads ask for a program of over 1 and up to 2 years in one of the fields listed above. Before you enrol, check that the program's length and field match the wording in the ads you want. Check the school's site for cost and start dates.

A short course. Durham College runs a shorter course for people who want this work: the Ontario Provincial and Bylaw Officer Core Competency Program. Its page says it takes about 36 hours, online and self-directed, with no prerequisite. It ends with an online final exam. The page says its certificates eventually expire. The course was waitlisted when checked on November 27, 2026.

MLEOA certification. The MLEOA runs a certification program. The MLEOA says:

  • it is the only organization authorized to certify municipal law enforcement officers in Ontario, under an Ontario Act passed in 1997,
  • certification is for its members who have reached a set standard of professional development, and
  • the designation has become a standard requirement for many employers.

Its certification page lists categories such as MLEOA membership and courses, the MLE Foundations Course or its challenge exam, and a law or business diploma or degree. Neither Brampton ad in this guide lists MLEOA certification as a requirement, so read each ad.

The hiring steps

The Brampton ads describe these steps but do not say in what order. Other employers may add or skip some.

  1. Apply online. You apply through the City's careers site and complete a questionnaire attached to the posting.
  2. Tests or exams. The ads say "various tests and/or exams may be administered."
  3. Interview. The interview may be done by video. Only people chosen for an interview are contacted.
  4. Background check. The person who gets the job needs a criminal record search. The ads say it checks for a criminal record that has not been pardoned.

Brampton sends test bookings and interview dates by email. Its ads say that if you do not reply, it will assume you are no longer interested and remove your application. Use an email address you check every day.

Tips if you are switching careers

Some skills in these ads, like writing records and dealing with upset people, may already be part of your current job. Here is how to use what you have:

  • Read the requirements line by line. Show how you meet each one in your resume.
  • Write about records. Both ads ask officers to document evidence. If your job involves reports, logs or incident notes, say so.
  • Show calm with upset people. Give one short, true example of handling a difficult customer or tenant.
  • Keep your driving record clean. Both ads require a non-probationary Class G licence, and the property standards ad asks for a clear driving record.

How to find by-law jobs

By-law jobs do not always say "by-law" in the title. Search for these words too:

  • enforcement,
  • property standards or municipal standards,
  • parking enforcement,
  • animal services or animal control, and
  • licensing or compliance.

Frequently asked questions

Is a by-law officer a police officer in Ontario?

No. The Community Safety and Policing Act says the term "police officer" does not include a municipal by-law enforcement officer. The Act says by-law officers are peace officers "for the purpose of enforcing municipal by-laws."

Do I need a certificate to become a by-law officer?

It depends on the employer. Neither Brampton ad in this guide asks for a certificate. The MLEOA certification is for its members, and the MLEOA says many employers require it.

How much do by-law officers make in the GTA?

Two City of Brampton postings open in November 2026 paid $35.44 an hour (Jr Property Standards Officer) and $31.75 an hour (Parking and Sign Enforcement Officer). Check each posting for its pay.

Do by-law officers go to court?

Yes, in both Brampton jobs. Both ads list testifying in court as part of the job. The parking ad also lists hearings and tribunals.

Do I need a driver's licence?

Both Brampton ads require a valid non-probationary Class G licence and the ability to get a City of Brampton permit. The property standards ad calls it a driver's permit and also asks for a clear driving record.
