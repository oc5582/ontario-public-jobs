To get a federal government job in the GTA, you create a GC Jobs account and apply to a job ad. You answer screening questions with real examples. If you are screened in, you may take a test, an interview or both. Then you give references and pass a security check. Many federal ads build a pool or an inventory. Managers may hire from a pool months or years later.

This guide uses the Public Service Commission of Canada's pages for applicants and live federal job ads. All facts were checked on November 22, 2026. For how federal hiring compares with Ontario and the City of Toronto, see our guide to how to get a government job in Toronto.

Key takeaways

  • The Public Service Commission's applicant pages tell you to apply through a GC Jobs account, online, before the closing date and time.
  • If you answer "yes" to a screening question, give a full example. A plain "yes" can screen you out.
  • Many ads build a pool or an inventory. Applying to an inventory means you are not applying for one specific job.
  • Each federal job ad states a language requirement. Bilingual jobs use levels A, B and C for reading, writing and oral skills.
  • Everyone needs at least Reliability Status before starting. Top Secret clearance is only for Canadian citizens.
  • The Public Service Commission says each step can take 4 weeks to a few months. The median time to hire was 227 days in 2021.
  • On November 22, 2026, PublicJobs.ca listed 44 open jobs at 16 federal departments and agencies, not counting CSIS. 31 of them close in 2027 or later.
  • PublicJobs.ca is independent and not affiliated with the Government of Canada. You apply on the government's own site.

Step 1: Create one GC Jobs account

The Public Service Commission's before you apply page includes these tips:

  • Use a personal email address.
  • Create only 1 account. You cannot merge accounts later.
  • Set up a GC Jobs alert, so you see new job ads.

If you studied outside Canada, the same page says to have your diplomas assessed against Canadian standards by a recognized credential assessment service.

Step 2: Read the job ad before you apply

Federal job ads usually have these parts. Here is what each one tells you, using a Transport Canada ad for a Senior Marine Safety Inspector inventory in North York as the example:

Watch the time zone. The Transport Canada, Public Services and Procurement Canada and RCMP ads all close at 23:59 Pacific Time, which is 2:59 a.m. the next morning in Toronto. Still, apply early. The Commission says GC Jobs sometimes has downtime.

Check who gets first choice. In jobs open to the public, the Public Service Employment Act gives preference to eligible veterans first, then to Canadian citizens and permanent residents, if they meet the essential qualifications. People with a priority entitlement come first of all, per the Commission's page on preference.

Step 3: Answer the screening questions with real examples

Federal applications often ask screening questions. For example (ours, not the Commission's): "Do you have experience leading a team?" The Commission's how to apply page gives this advice:

  • Use the exact keywords from the question. Do not use synonyms.
  • Write "I", not "we".
  • If you answer "yes", write a full answer in the box below to prove it. The STAR method helps: Situation, Task, Action, Result.
  • If the question says "and", show every part. If it says "or", one part is enough.

The Transport Canada ad says it plainly: it is "not sufficient" to only state that you meet a qualification. Give details such as when, where and how.

The RCMP's clerical inventory ad forbids using artificial intelligence in the application and assessments.

The Commission also says to paste a very simple resume with no bullets or bold, because GC Jobs removes most formatting, and to set aside 1 to 3 hours per application. You can edit your application until the closing date, but you must submit it again before the closing date, or it will not be considered.

Step 4: Check if the ad builds a pool or an inventory

One federal ad can lead to many jobs. The Commission's what to expect page says managers use pools and inventories, which are lists of candidates they can hire from over time. It tells you to read the "Intent of the process" to see if a pool is being created, and to apply to several jobs.

In the ads we checked, it works as a sequence:

  1. You apply to an inventory. The Transport Canada ad says: "you are not applying for a specific job, but to an inventory for future vacancies." There, your application stays active for 180 days. You get a notice 30 days before it ends and can renew it.
  2. The hiring team pulls applications to assess. The Public Services and Procurement Canada ad for a Geomatics Officer in Toronto calls this an "extraction". Its first one is on December 7, 2026, although the ad closes on March 31, 2027. Applying early can matter.
  3. People found qualified can go into a pool. The Transport Canada ad may create one for similar jobs in the Ontario Region. The PSPC ad builds a fully assessed pool.
  4. Managers hire from the pool later. The Commission's what happens next page says pools usually last a year or more, and managers may hire from them months or years later. Being in a pool does not mean you will get a job offer.

This may be why many federal ads stay open a long time. Of the 44 federal jobs on PublicJobs.ca on November 22, 2026, 31 had a closing date in 2027 or later, and 17 had "inventory" in the job title.

Step 5: Tests and interviews

After screening, check your GC Jobs account. If you are screened in, you may write a test or a series of tests, and you may be invited to an interview, per the Commission's testing page. Your invitation lists the qualifications being assessed.

The Commission's interview tips: prepare a STAR example for each qualification on the invitation, talk about your own role, and link your skills to the department's priorities. Questions test competencies, which can be behavioural, like adaptability, or technical, like data analysis. Our TTC hiring process guide explains how to use STAR.

If you need an accommodation, such as an alternative format, tell the person who invites you as soon as possible.

Language requirements and second language tests

The Commission says on its what to expect page that all federal positions have language requirements: English, French or both. The language requirements for candidates page explains the labels:

  • English essential or French essential means you need only that language. The Transport Canada ad is English essential.
  • Bilingual means you need both. The ad gives a profile with a level for reading, writing and oral interaction: A (beginner), B (intermediate) or C (advanced). A CBC profile means C in reading, B in writing and C in oral.
  • Imperative means you must meet the level when you are hired. Non-imperative means you can be hired if you agree to take language training at government expense.

Second language tests check your level. The Commission says results for its reading test are valid for 5 years. It also offers self-assessment tests for reading and writing.

Step 6: References and security screening

The Commission lists giving references to the manager and going through security screening as one step. Before you start any federal job, you need a security check. The Commission's what happens next page lists three levels:

Level What it allows History you provide
Reliability Status Minimum for all federal jobs 5 years
Secret Access up to Secret information 10 years
Top Secret Access up to Top Secret information. Canadian citizens only 10 years

You give 2 pieces of ID, one with a photo. The form asks for past addresses, schools, jobs, close contacts and any criminal record. Fingerprints are mandatory, and there is a credit check. If you lived outside Canada for more than 6 months in a row in that period, you may be asked for more information. The Commission says to start collecting this information early and be honest.

How long federal hiring takes

Plan for months. The Commission says each step can take 4 weeks to a few months. It also says the median time to hire a new candidate was 227 days in 2021.

Only people selected for the next step are contacted, so keep applying.

Pay, job types and pension

Pay. Pay follows the job's group and level, shown on the ad. The RCMP clerical inventory pays $57,217 to $69,106. The Transport Canada marine inspector pays $112,823 to $131,504.

Job types. The Commission says permanent jobs are called indeterminate. Shorter jobs are term or casual. An Indigenous Services Canada casual inventory defines casual work as 90 working days or less in a calendar year.

Office presence. The Public Services and Procurement Canada ad says the required in-office presence is currently four days a week.

Pension. The Treasury Board's 2024 report calls the Public Service Pension Plan a defined benefit plan. Check your offer to see if your job is covered. The basic formula is 2% per year of service times the average of your 5 highest-paid consecutive years, up to 35 years of service.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to speak French to work for the federal government?

Not always. Some jobs are English essential, such as the Transport Canada ad above. Bilingual jobs give a language profile, such as CBC (C in reading, B in writing, C in oral).

What is the difference between a pool and an inventory?

In the ads we checked, you apply to an inventory for future jobs. The hiring team pulls applications from it to assess. People found qualified may then be placed in a pool that managers hire from later.

Who can apply to a federal job ad?

Each ad has a "Who can apply" line. The Transport Canada ad was open to "persons residing in Canada". In jobs open to the public, citizens and permanent residents get preference after veterans. PublicJobs.ca does not give eligibility advice.
