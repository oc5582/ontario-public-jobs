The City of Burlington hires through an online job portal run on Workday. You make an account, answer the questions and upload a resume and cover letter. The City contacts only the people it invites to an interview. Interviews are behavioural, with a panel of usually two or three people, in person or on Zoom.

This guide explains each step using the City's own careers pages and the 20 City of Burlington postings that were open on PublicJobs.ca on November 12, 2026. All facts were checked that day.

Key takeaways

  • You apply online through the City's Workday job portal. The City says it no longer accepts paper resumes.
  • The City says it will only contact applicants who are invited to an interview. If the City does not contact you, you were most likely not selected.
  • Interviews are behavioural. Panels usually have two or three people, and virtual interviews use Zoom.
  • Some jobs add a written, oral or practical exam. 8 of the 20 postings asked for a clear criminal record check, paid for by the person hired.
  • All 20 open postings showed pay on November 12, 2026, from $18.21 an hour (part-time recreation) to $174,918 a year (a solicitor).
  • The City says it matches employee contributions to the OMERS pension 100%.
  • PublicJobs.ca is independent and not affiliated with the City of Burlington. You apply on the City's own site.

Who the City of Burlington employs

The City's Why Work For Us page says it has about 1,150 full-time and about 1,000 part-time employees. They deliver more than 37 services.

The jobs open on November 12, 2026 show that spread:

  • Office and professional: Application Analyst, Financial Analyst, Planner, Solicitor, Manager of Insurance and Risk Services.
  • Hands-on and trades: Roads Foreperson, Heavy Equipment Operator, 310T Truck and Coach Mechanic.
  • Transit: Scheduler and Manager of Transit Operations. Burlington Transit is part of the City.
  • Part-time recreation and facilities: program staff, an aquafit instructor, arena and pool operators, and facility attendants.

Of the 20 postings, 14 were full-time and 6 were part-time. You can see them all on the City of Burlington employer page on PublicJobs.ca. Each one links to the City's own posting.

Step 1: Find a posting and apply online

Workday is the hiring system the City uses for its job portal. The City's How to Apply page sets out the rules:

  • Apply online. The page says you must apply online. Paper resumes are no longer accepted.
  • Make an account first. Go to the City's job portal, click Sign In at the top right, and create an account with your email and a password.
  • Browser problems. If sign-up fails on an iPhone, iPad or Safari on a computer, the City suggests trying another browser.
  • What you send. You answer the required questions and upload a resume, a cover letter and any other documents that support your application.
  • Job alerts. The same account lets you set up alerts for new postings.

The City does not accept unsolicited resumes. You must apply to a specific posting.

If you already work for the City

Current employees apply through the Jobs Hub in their own City Workday account. The How to Apply page says that is how they are considered as internal applicants.

Step 2: Wait to hear back

Every posting ends with the same note. The City thanks all applicants, but says only those invited for an interview will be contacted. The How to Apply page says the same.

So if you hear nothing, you were most likely not selected for that job. The City's careers pages do not say how long you should wait. Keep applying to other postings that fit.

Step 3: Tests for some jobs

Some jobs add an exam as part of the hiring process. On November 12, 2026, the Foreperson Roads posting said candidates will be required to pass a "written, oral and/or practical examination." The Heavy Equipment Operator posting said candidates may be required to pass one.

The City's careers pages do not list which jobs have tests. Check the Additional Information section of each posting.

Step 4: The panel interview

The City's Interview Process page explains what to expect.

Format. The City uses a hybrid model, so interviews can be in person or virtual. Virtual interviews use Zoom. The page says the questions are shown in the Zoom chat, so you can scroll back and read a question again.

The panel. Panels usually have two or three people. That is often the job's supervisor, plus someone from Human Resources and/or one or two people from the work area.

The questions. The City uses behavioural questions. A behavioural question is a question about how you handled something in the past. For example (our example, not the City's): "Tell us about a time you had to fix a mistake quickly." The City says your past examples are a good predictor of how you may handle a similar situation in the future.

Questions may also cover:

  • your skills, knowledge and attitude,
  • your past jobs and related experience,
  • what you know about the job and the City, and
  • your career goals and education.

How the City says to prepare

The Interview Process page gives clear advice. In short:

  1. Print the posting. List the skills it asks for.
  2. Think of times you used each skill.
  3. Prepare at least three short, detailed stories. Each one should say what the situation was, what you did and what the result was. Use numbers if you can.
  4. Prepare a story about something that did not go to plan, and what you learned.
  5. Your stories can come from volunteer work, school or hobbies, not only paid jobs.
  6. Say "I", not "we". The panel wants to know what you did.
  7. Be ready to give three professional references.
  8. Arrive 5 to 10 minutes early. For Zoom, test the link, your internet, microphone and camera first.

That story pattern is close to STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Our TTC hiring process guide explains STAR in more detail. Our City of Toronto hiring guide covers panel interviews at another GTA municipality.

Asking for accommodation

Accommodation means a change to how you apply or interview, for example because of a disability. The City says it accommodates applicants with disabilities under the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act. Call 905-335-7602 or email hr@burlington.ca. The Interview Process page says it is your choice whether to share a disability.

Step 5: Checks before you start

Postings list the conditions you must meet. These came up most often on November 12, 2026:

  • Criminal record check. 8 of the 20 postings asked the person hired to give a clear criminal record check at their own expense. The 3 recreation program jobs also asked for a vulnerable sector check.
  • Driver's licence and abstract. 5 postings, including the roads, mechanic and arena jobs, set a driving record limit. Most say no more than three driving convictions or three demerit points in the three years before the hire date, and some add no licence suspensions. The licence class depends on the job, such as G or DZ.
  • What an abstract is. A driver's abstract is a record of your driving history. Check the exact driving rule on your posting.
  • Certificates. Two part-time facility jobs ask for WHMIS and first aid certificates. The Facility Operations Attendant posting accepts proof that you will complete them as a condition of the job.

The City's careers pages do not describe the reference check or offer stage. Ask HR if a posting is not clear.

Unions

CUPE is the Canadian Union of Public Employees. Three of its locals appeared on the postings: CUPE Local 44 (roads and arena jobs), CUPE Local 2723 (the transit mechanic job) and CUPE Local 1540 (the Senior Design Technician job). Three part-time postings said "Non Union". Many professional and management postings listed no union.

Pension and hybrid work

The Why Work For Us page says the City gives 100% employer matched contributions to the OMERS pension. OMERS is a defined benefit pension plan for Ontario's municipal sector. Check each posting and ask HR whether your job type joins the plan.

The City calls itself a hybrid workplace. But 7 hybrid postings on November 12, 2026 said that, starting in September 2027, the job must be on site at least 50% of the time each month. Read the Location section before you apply.

How long does City of Burlington hiring take?

The City's careers pages do not give a timeline. Here is what the postings show, based on the posted and closing dates on PublicJobs.ca:

  • Full-time postings close fast. 9 of the 14 full-time postings open on November 12 were open for only 14 or 15 days. The Manager, Transit Operations job was open for 11 days (November 2 to 13).
  • Part-time postings stay open longer. Several were posted in June or September 2026 and close on December 31, 2026 or later.

Set up job alerts on the City's portal, or check the board often, so a two-week window does not pass you by.

Frequently asked questions

Will the City tell me if I did not get the job?

The City says it only contacts applicants invited to an interview. If you are not invited, you will most likely not hear back.

Are City of Burlington interviews in person or online?

Both. The City says interviews can be in person or virtual, and virtual interviews use Zoom.

Do I need a police check?

Some jobs need one. 8 of the 20 postings on November 12, 2026 asked the person hired for a clear criminal record check at their own expense. Recreation program jobs also asked for a vulnerable sector check.

Is Burlington Transit part of the City?

Yes. Burlington Transit jobs, such as Scheduler and Mechanic, are posted on the City of Burlington's job portal.

Summary

  • Make an account on the City's Workday portal and set up job alerts.
  • Read each posting's facts, Requirements and Location sections before you write.
  • Prepare at least three short stories for a behavioural panel interview, plus three references.
  • Expect a test for some jobs, and checks such as a criminal record check or driver's abstract.
  • Apply quickly: many full-time postings close in about two weeks.
