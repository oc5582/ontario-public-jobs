The Ontario Public Service (OPS) hires in 5 steps. It advertises the job for at least 10 working days, screens each application received on time, and assesses the selected applicants. That usually means a panel interview. It can also include a test, a written assignment or a presentation. Then it picks the top candidate and makes an offer. Some jobs also need a security screening check before you start.

Each step below comes from Ontario's own careers pages, checked on November 16, 2026.

Key takeaways

  • Ontario lists 5 hiring steps: job advertising, application screening, evaluation, selection and offer.
  • Your cover letter and resume together should be no longer than 5 pages, in one file.
  • Panel interviews are the typical format, with 2 to 4 evaluators. Every candidate gets the same questions, and each answer is scored against an expected answer.
  • If the job ad lists a security screening check, you need a clearance before you start. Ontario says a record does not automatically rule you out.
  • Pay is posted by the hour, the week or the year, depending on the job.
  • On November 14, 2026, PublicJobs.ca listed 39 open OPS jobs. 33 of them showed pay.
  • PublicJobs.ca is independent and not affiliated with the Government of Ontario. You apply the way each job ad says, usually on the province's careers site.

What the Ontario Public Service is

The Ontario Public Service is the workforce of the Government of Ontario. Ontario's About the Ontario Public Service page says the OPS has more than 60,000 public servants.

Each job ad names the ministry or agency the position belongs to. On November 14, 2026, 33 of the 39 OPS jobs on PublicJobs.ca were in 13 ministries, such as the Ministry of Transportation and the Ministry of the Attorney General. The other 6 were in 5 agencies, including the Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario and the Ontario Science Centre.

Who can apply

Ontario's hiring process page lists three basic requirements. You must:

  • be a Canadian citizen, a permanent resident, or authorized in writing to work in Canada,
  • be over 16, and
  • have a Social Insurance Number.

Not every job ad is open to everyone. Ontario's job ad definitions page explains the four posting statuses:

Posting status Who can apply
Open All qualified applicants
Open Targeted All qualified applicants. The ad is promoted to specific audiences
Restricted Internal OPS applicants only
Restricted (non-represented) Internal OPS applicants and Canadian Armed Forces veterans

The 5 steps, from job ad to offer

The hiring process page lists these steps:

Step What happens
1. Job advertising The job is posted for at least 10 working days. Applications close at 11:59 p.m. Eastern time on the closing date
2. Application screening Every application received on time is screened and rated against the qualifications in the job ad
3. Evaluation Selected applicants do assessments, usually including an oral interview
4. Selection The top candidate or candidates are chosen
5. Offer A verbal offer first, then a written offer letter

Step 1: Read the job ad closely

Each OPS job ad has a Job ID. Ontario says to include this number in your application. The ad also shows whether the job is permanent or temporary. A temporary job has an end date.

Of the 39 OPS jobs on PublicJobs.ca on November 14, 2026, 21 were temporary, 9 were permanent and 4 offered both. The other 5 did not show a type.

The Qualifications section is what you are measured against. The definitions page says your application must clearly show how you meet the qualifications, and the selection process measures candidates against them.

Two phrases in OPS ads are worth knowing:

  • "Candidates from this job posting may be used to fill similar positions". Ontario says applicants who progress through this competition may be eligible for similar jobs for the next 12 months. The jobs can be in the same ministry or another one, within 125 km of the work location.
  • "Less qualified applicants may be considered on an underfill basis". If no applicant is fully qualified, the OPS may consider someone who is not. That person is offered a lower classification and a lower pay rate.

Step 2: Send an application that fits the ad

Ontario's instructions are specific:

  • Length. Your cover letter and resume combined should not exceed 5 pages.
  • One file. Attach both as one PDF, Word, plain text or rich-text file.
  • Online. Most competitions need an online application. Enter your email address to get a confirmation email.
  • Past OPS jobs. If you worked for the OPS before, you must say so in your cover letter or resume. List the positions, the dates and any active restriction on being rehired.

Ontario also publishes a cover letter and resume writing guide. It is dated October 2015. It suggests:

  • a cover letter of 1 to 2 pages and a resume of 2 to 3 pages,
  • a subject line with the Job ID, the job title and the ministry,
  • matching your skills to each qualification in the ad, with a real example, and
  • leaving out personal details such as your date of birth and Social Insurance Number.

The guide says a cover letter is not mandatory, but calls it the ideal place to show how you meet the qualifications. It also says the OPS does not use your reasons for wanting the job as screening criteria.

Steps 3 and 4: Tests and the panel interview

Hearing back. Ontario says only candidates invited to continue will be contacted, by phone or email. If many applicants pass the first screen, the first contact may be a screening test or a phone interview.

The panel. Ontario says a panel interview is the typical format. A panel has 2 to 4 evaluators, including the manager of the position. They write or type while you talk, so Ontario suggests speaking slowly.

The scoring. Each candidate gets the same questions. Each question has a set weight. The panel compares your answer with an expected answer to give a score. Ontario says more detailed answers raise your chances.

The questions. The hiring process page describes four types:

Type What it asks
General Why you want the job and how your background fits
Technical What you know about the programs, policies or procedures of the job
Situational What you would do in a made-up work situation
Behavioural A real example of how you used a skill before

Ontario says behavioural questions are the most common in OPS interviews. One of its sample questions: "Tell me about a time when you had to design a solution to a key problem facing your work unit. What did you do, and what was the result?" It suggests the PAR method: the Problem you faced, the Action you took and the Result. Our TTC hiring process guide explains a similar method, STAR.

Other assessments. Ontario lists a writing test, a technical test, a work sample, a presentation, a role-play and an in-box exercise.

How to prepare. Ontario suggests reading the most recent Ontario Budget and Speech from the Throne, and the ministry's website.

Accommodation. Ontario says you have the right to request accommodation during the recruitment process.

References and the security screening check

References. Reference checks are part of the process. Ontario expects the names of current and former supervisors who can speak about your work. Ask them first.

Security screening. Not every job needs it. When the job ad lists an employment security screening check, you need an OPS security clearance before you start work. In some cases the offer is conditional on the clearance. Ontario says the process may include:

  • checking your identity with photo ID,
  • a police record check,
  • other record checks if they apply, such as a driving history search, and
  • a background check with employers from the last five years and your references, if it applies.

The definitions page names the types of police check: a Criminal Record Check, a Criminal Record and Judicial Matters Check, a Vulnerable Sector Check, and other checks the law allows. It says a record does not automatically make you ineligible. Results are weighed against the duties of the job and kept confidential.

Step 5: The offer, pay and unions

The offer. The first offer is verbal. If you accept, the written offer letter gives your start date, classification and starting salary. If relevant, it also names the bargaining unit and bargaining agent.

How pay is posted. Ontario says salary ranges appear in three formats: hourly, weekly or annual. The 33 OPS jobs showing pay on PublicJobs.ca on November 14, 2026 used all three:

Format Jobs Example
Annual 17 Senior Policy Advisor, Ministry of Health: $86,379 to $127,288 a year
Hourly 9 Legal Assistant, Ministry of the Attorney General: $28.14 to $32.82 an hour
Weekly 7 Transportation Enforcement Officer, Ministry of Transportation: $1,453.74 to $1,710.08 a week

The lowest starting rate was $26.98 an hour, for a Maintenance Mechanic. The highest top rate was $191,716 a year, for a Senior Manager at the Ontario Financing Authority. The other 6 jobs were lawyer roles with "salary commensurate with legal experience".

Hours. The ad's Schedule sets the hours. Schedules 3 and 3.7 are 36.25 hours a week. Schedules 4 and 4.7 are 40 hours. Schedule 6 is a minimum of 36.25 hours.

Unions. Each job belongs to a compensation group. Ontario says OPS staff may be represented by one of eight bargaining agents. It calls OPSEU the largest bargaining unit and AMAPCEO the second-largest bargaining agent.

Pensions. The hiring process page does not describe the pension. The Ontario Pension Board says it administers the Public Service Pension Plan. It says its members are certain employees of the provincial government and its agencies, boards and commissions. OPTrust says it administers the OPSEU Pension Plan. Check the job ad, or ask the hiring manager, whether the job comes with a pension plan and which one.

How long OPS hiring takes

Ontario's careers pages do not give a total time. What they do say:

  • Jobs are posted for at least 10 working days. On PublicJobs.ca, the 34 OPS jobs showing both dates had 12 to 28 calendar days between the Posted and Closes dates shown there. The shortest was an Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario job that takes applications on its own careers site.
  • You can check the competition status with the link in your confirmation email, or by searching the Job ID on the OPS careers site. If you see only the job ad, screening has not started.

Where to find OPS jobs

Each job ad says how to apply. Most use the province's own careers site. A few name another route, such as an agency's own careers site or an application form. Start at Ontario's Careers: Ontario Public Service page and use its Search job openings link. PublicJobs.ca lists Ontario Public Service jobs and links each one to the employer's own posting. Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be a Canadian citizen to work for the Ontario Public Service?

Ontario's hiring process page lists three options: Canadian citizen, permanent resident of Canada, or authorized in writing to work in Canada. Read the job ad for any other mandatory requirements, and check the posting status.

What is an OPS interview like?

A panel of 2 to 4 evaluators, including the manager of the position, asks every candidate the same scored questions.

Does every OPS job need a security check?

No. Only when the job ad lists an employment security screening check.

Can one application lead to other OPS jobs?

Sometimes. If the ad says candidates may be used to fill similar positions, Ontario says you may be eligible for similar jobs within 125 km for the next 12 months, if you progress through the selection process.

Does the OPS have a program for new graduates?

Yes. Ontario's students and new professionals page lists internships, co-ops and summer jobs. The Ontario Internship Program is a paid, one-year placement for recent graduates.
