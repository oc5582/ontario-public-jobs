The Canadian Security Intelligence Service (CSIS) hires professionals in 10 steps. You apply online, answer a short questionnaire, and have a competency-based interview. Then come references, a psychological assessment and an Enhanced Top Secret security clearance with a polygraph test. You get a letter of offer only after the clearance. CSIS says the process takes about 6 to 18 months after the first interview.

This guide explains each step using CSIS's own careers pages and job postings. All facts were checked on November 13, 2026.

Key takeaways

  • CSIS only accepts applications through its careers site, Canada.ca/csisjobs. Applicants must be Canadian citizens.
  • CSIS lists 10 steps for professionals. The polygraph is part of the security clearance, and CSIS says it is mandatory for every employee.
  • CSIS says the process takes about 6 to 18 months after the first interview.
  • CSIS checks 10 years of your addresses, work, education and travel. A clearance from another job does not transfer.
  • Most pay follows CSIS's 11 levels. On November 13, 2026, pay on the 9 open postings ran from $61,934 to $202,918 a year.
  • On November 13, 2026, 5 of the 9 open postings named Toronto as a possible location. Most CSIS work must be done in the office.
  • PublicJobs.ca is independent and not affiliated with CSIS. You apply on CSIS's own site.

What CSIS is, and what it hires for

CSIS is Canada's security intelligence agency. It is a federal employer, but every CSIS posting we checked says CSIS is a separate employer with its own classification, pay system and staffing process.

The CSIS Available Jobs page says it hires for more than 100 specializations. On November 13, 2026, it linked to 10 postings for professionals and 11 co-op student streams. One of the 10, Editor, had closed on November 6, so 9 were open.

None of them was an Intelligence Officer or Surveillance Officer job. They ranged from Administrative Assistant to an executive Director General post.

Who can apply

CSIS's page on how to submit a powerful application lists the basic criteria. CSIS says applications that do not meet them are rejected automatically. You must:

  • be a Canadian citizen,
  • be 18 or older,
  • be able to give 10 years of information that CSIS can verify,
  • follow CSIS's rules on drug use, and
  • be available for every recruitment step. All steps take place in Canada.

The Recruitment Process page adds that some jobs need a valid, permanent driver's licence, and some list mobility as a requirement.

The CSIS jobs FAQ adds some detail. Permanent residents cannot apply. People born abroad and people with dual citizenship can, if they are Canadian citizens. There is no rule about how long you have lived in Canada. But CSIS must be able to verify where you lived, worked, studied and travelled for the past 10 years.

PublicJobs.ca does not give immigration or eligibility advice. If you are not sure, read the posting or ask a CSIS recruiter at one of its career events.

The 10 steps, from application to your first day

CSIS lists these steps on its Recruitment Process page for professionals:

Step What happens
1. Apply online Resume and cover letter on Canada.ca/csisjobs
2. Pre-screening A short questionnaire to confirm you are eligible
3. Interview A competency-based interview with the hiring manager(s) and an HR advisor
4. Employment package More documents, including 3 references
5. Language test A second language evaluation, if the job needs one
6. Psychological assessment Includes an interview with a psychologist
7. File review A short questionnaire to check your information before the clearance
8. Security clearance Background documents, fingerprints, a security interview and a polygraph
9. Letter of offer Sent once your clearance is granted
10. Start work New employees start with 3 weeks of vacation

Some jobs add tests. The IT Analyst posting said an exam may be used to check technical knowledge. The Communications Analyst job, which translates foreign-language material, has three assessments in one full-day evaluation.

Step 1: Apply online

The FAQ says applications are only accepted on Canada.ca/csisjobs, even though CSIS also posts its jobs on other federal job sites. It strongly recommends applying online, because the online application includes a questionnaire that cannot be sent to you.

What CSIS wants in your cover letter

  • Length: no more than 2 to 3 pages.
  • Subject line: the Reference Number and Position Title from the posting.
  • First section: why you want this job, and why at CSIS.
  • Second section: how you meet each language, education and experience requirement. Give a real example for each one.
  • Third section: which assets you have, with an example for each. Assets are "nice to have" skills, not requirements.
  • Location: if the job is in more than one city, say which ones you would work in, in order of preference.

All 10 postings we checked warn that you will be screened out if you do not clearly show how you meet each education and experience requirement.

What CSIS wants in your resume

CSIS asks for an Education section and a Professional Experience section. For each job, list the employer, location, title, start and end dates (month and year), duties and achievements. Say whether it was full-time or part-time. CSIS counts 37.5 hours a week or more as full-time. CSIS asks you to keep the resume to 10 pages at most.

If you studied outside Canada, CSIS asks for proof of Canadian equivalency from a credential assessment service, usually at your own cost.

Steps 2 to 7: Screening, interview and assessments

Hearing back. CSIS says it only contacts people selected to move forward. It does not give feedback to people who are not selected. Messages may come from an @smtp.gc.ca or @hiringplatform.com address. CSIS also says its messages will name Public Safety Canada, not CSIS, to keep your application private.

Keep it quiet. All 10 postings we checked ask you not to discuss your application with anyone except your partner or close family, including on social media.

The interview. The interview is competency-based. That means you answer questions with real examples of how you used a skill. Each of the 10 postings lists the competencies it will assess, such as communication, judgement or problem solving. CSIS suggests you research the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Our TTC hiring process guide explains STAR with an example.

Language. Some CSIS jobs are bilingual and some need English only (English Essential). The posting gives the language requirement. CSIS arranges the second language evaluation itself.

Paperwork. After the interview you send an employment package with 3 references. Before the clearance, a short file review questionnaire checks that your information is accurate.

Psychological assessment. This step includes an interview with a psychologist. CSIS's pages do not describe what it covers.

Step 8: The Top Secret clearance and polygraph

Every CSIS employee needs a Top Secret clearance, the FAQ says, and the polygraph is a mandatory part of it. The postings call it an Enhanced Top Secret security clearance.

The postings say the clearance includes:

  • a security interview,
  • a polygraph test, and
  • a background check, including credit and financial checks.

The FAQ explains the background check. You fill in security forms about your addresses, work and travel for the past 10 years. CSIS then checks your activities in Canada and abroad.

What the FAQ and postings say about common worries:

  • An existing clearance. It does not transfer. CSIS may look at it, but you still do the forms, the security interview and the polygraph.
  • Debts or bankruptcy. Personal finances are reviewed. Each case is assessed on its own.
  • A criminal record. It does not stop you from applying. CSIS says to be upfront and honest from your first interview.
  • Traffic tickets. Many traffic violations may affect whether you can get the clearance.
  • Drugs. Postings say drug use is an important factor. They advise you not to use any illegal drugs from the day you apply.
  • Your current employer. You choose whether CSIS may contact them.

How long CSIS hiring takes

The FAQ says the process takes about 6 to 18 months after the first interview. It depends on several factors, including how many people are in the process. CSIS also says verifying travel and work history can be complex or lengthy, and suggests keeping records of significant time spent abroad.

CSIS also says:

  • It reviews applications as they come in, so apply early. The postings say screening is on a rolling basis until positions are filled.
  • You can apply for the same job again after 6 months.

For students, CSIS says to apply about a year before the work term, because processing a student application and getting the clearance takes about 10 to 12 months.

The Director General and Communications Advisor postings had a closing date of November 13, 2026. A few things to know about pay:

  • Where you start. CSIS sets your salary inside the posted range, based on your education and experience. It cannot pay above the range.
  • IT jobs. The IT Analyst posting said it qualifies for a Technology Market Modifier worth 7% of base salary.
  • Benefits. CSIS lists 3 weeks of vacation to start, sick leave credits, life, health and dental coverage, and a pension. The FAQ sends pension questions to the Public Service Pension Centre.
  • Probation. New employees have at least one year of probation. The letter of offer gives the exact length.

What the work is like

Office work. The FAQ says CSIS jobs must be done at one of its offices, not from home. Recent postings say most roles need an in-office presence.

Shifts. The Regional Protective Services Officer job lists shift work, nights, weekends, overtime and a uniform. Some IT jobs work rotating shifts in a 24/7 setting.

Relocation. The FAQ says Intelligence Officer is the only CSIS job that requires moving. New officers train and then complete a three-year development program at headquarters in Ottawa. They must be willing to relocate during their career.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to take a polygraph to work at CSIS?

Yes. CSIS says every employee needs a Top Secret clearance, and the polygraph is a mandatory part of it.

How long is the CSIS hiring process?

CSIS says about 6 to 18 months after the first interview. You get the letter of offer only after your clearance is granted.

Can permanent residents apply to CSIS?

CSIS says no. You must be a Canadian citizen to work there. Dual citizens can apply.

Does CSIS hire in Toronto?

Yes, for some jobs. On November 13, 2026, 5 of the 9 open CSIS postings named Toronto as a possible location. Each posting lists its cities under Location.

Do I need a university degree?

Not always. CSIS's FAQ says most positions need a college diploma or a university degree. But 4 of the 9 open postings accepted a high school diploma plus experience.

Summary

  • Gather 10 years of addresses, jobs, schools and trips.
  • Plan for 6 to 18 months after the first interview, and keep applying elsewhere while you wait.
