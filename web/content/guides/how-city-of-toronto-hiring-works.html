The City of Toronto hires through its own careers site, jobs.toronto.ca. The City describes 5 hiring steps: apply online, an interview or test (or both), verification, job offer and onboarding. Recreation and Corporate Security jobs add extra steps. Firefighter, paramedic and some other jobs have their own recruitment pages.

This guide explains each step using the City's own careers pages, current City postings and the City's workforce numbers. Facts were last checked on October 29 and 30, 2026.

Key takeaways

  • You need a candidate profile on jobs.toronto.ca to apply. Applications close before 11:59 p.m. Eastern time on the closing date.
  • The City's 5 steps are: online application, interview and/or testing, verification, job offer, onboarding.
  • Before an offer is final, the City may check references, your driver's licence, your health, your education and your police record.
  • City postings such as Payroll Program Assistant 1 say your application "must describe your qualifications" as they relate to the Key Qualifications. Write to that list.
  • The City's hiring pages do not say how long hiring takes. Postings move fast: on October 29, 2026, 28 of 60 open City jobs on PublicJobs.ca were due to close within 7 days.
  • The TTC, Toronto Community Housing and the Toronto Zoo are separate employers. They do not hire through the City's site.
  • PublicJobs.ca is independent and not affiliated with the City of Toronto. You apply on the City's own site.

Step 1: Create a profile and apply online

You apply online at jobs.toronto.ca. First you make a candidate profile. Then you find a job and click Apply. PublicJobs.ca also lists current City of Toronto openings and links each one to the City's posting.

The City's How to Apply page says you must create a candidate profile before you can apply. It suggests making one now, even if you are not applying yet.

The Help page explains the details:

  • You can look at jobs without signing in. You must sign in to apply.
  • You upload your resume and cover letter under My Documents.
  • You can upload a different resume for each job. Your earlier applications do not change.
  • You can set up job alerts by keyword under Options. The City emails you when a matching job is posted.
  • You see your applications and their status under Jobs Applied.

Two rules about deadlines and email matter most:

  • Closing time. Applications must be in before 11:59 p.m. Eastern time on the day the posting closes.
  • Email. You must keep your contact details current and check your email often. The City sends messages about your application status there.

On October 30, 2026, the careers home page showed a notice about a problem with document uploads on profiles and applications. If an upload fails, email jobshelp@toronto.ca, the City's address for problems with the online application.

Can you change an application after you send it?

Yes, but only while the posting is still open. The Help page says you click Withdraw Application, update your information, then click Resubmit Application. You can also withdraw at any time while you are still being considered.

Before you apply: how to read a City posting

City postings open with a block of facts like the one below. Read it before you write anything. Here is what each line means, using two postings that were open on October 30, 2026.

Line on the posting What it tells you Example
Job ID The number to quote in emails to the City 67225
Job Type & Duration Permanent or temporary, and for how long Full-time, 3 permanent vacancies
Hourly Rate and Wage Grade (or Salary Range) The pay range and wage grade $40.08 to $43.91 an hour (Wage Grade 7910)
Shift Information Days and hours Monday to Friday, 35 hours a week
Affiliation The union, or "Non-Union" L79 Full-time
Number of Positions Open How many openings there are 3
Posting Period The open and close dates October 26 to November 9, 2026

The first example is a Payroll Program Assistant 1 with Toronto Fire Services. The second, an Administrative Assistant 2 with Toronto Building, shows a salary of $64,529 to $91,111 and says "Non-Union". It has 4 positions across City Hall and three civic centres.

"L79" means the job is in CUPE Local 79, the City's largest union. At the end of September 2026, 27,805 of the City's 44,335 active employees (63%) were in Local 79, per the City's workforce statistics. That count leaves out the City's agencies, boards and commissions. Another 7,435 employees were non-union.

Write to the Key Qualifications list

City postings include a Key Qualifications list. The payroll posting says your application must describe your qualifications as they relate to that list. So the list is your checklist.

The City's Job Application Tips give this advice:

  • Tailor a separate resume to each job.
  • Keep it to 2 or 3 pages.
  • Show what you achieved, not only your duties.
  • Leave out personal details such as date of birth or marital status.
  • Tell the truth on every line.

If your cover letter claims a skill, the tips say it should be in your resume too.

Step 2: Interview and/or testing

If you are selected, the City contacts you for an interview, a test, or both. The City's Hiring Process page does not say which jobs have tests. That depends on the job.

The Help page says the City gets a high volume of applications and cannot give individual status updates. Check Jobs Applied and your email instead.

When the City calls, the Hiring Process page suggests a few things:

  • Write down the time and place.
  • Ask for any accommodation you need. This means a change to how the interview or test is done, for example because of a disability.
  • Ask for the start and end dates if the job is temporary.
  • Ask where the job is.

What the interview is like

The Job Application Tips say to expect a panel. That means more than one interviewer. They ask specific, behavioural questions. A behavioural question asks how you handled something in the past. For example (our example, not the City's): "Tell us about a time you missed a deadline."

The tips also give this advice for before and on the day:

  • Research the job, the division's values and its plans first.
  • Arrive early, because there may be paperwork.
  • Bring your references.
  • Answer clearly and briefly. Ask for a question to be repeated if you need it.
  • At the end, ask about next steps and thank the panel.

The City's tips do not name a method for answering. STAR, which the TTC recommends in its interview guide, is one simple way: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Our TTC hiring process guide explains STAR and the four question types TTC panels use.

Step 3: Verification before the offer

Passing the interview is not the end. The Hiring Process page says a job offer may depend on:

  1. a reference check,
  2. a driver's licence check,
  3. an occupational health assessment,
  4. a check of your education, and
  5. a police reference check.

The page says an offer "may" depend on these checks, so they vary by job. If a posting does not say which apply, ask the City.

Have your references ready before you apply. Ask them first, and make sure their phone numbers and emails are current.

Step 4: The job offer

The City sends a written offer. The Hiring Process page says to read it in detail and ask questions before you accept and sign.

It also says to tell the City's Strategic Recruitment team if your Social Insurance Number begins with 9. PublicJobs.ca cannot advise on eligibility. If you have questions, ask the City.

Step 5: Onboarding

New staff get an orientation for their job. They also attend the City's Corporate New Employee Orientation, booked through the City's learning system.

How long does City of Toronto hiring take?

The City's Hiring Process, How to Apply and Help pages do not give a timeline. Here is what can be checked:

  • Posting windows are short. The two postings above were open for 14 days each. PublicJobs.ca's job stats showed 60 open City of Toronto jobs on October 29, 2026, and 28 of them were due to close within the next 7 days.
  • Recreation can take weeks. The City's recreation hiring page says it could be weeks between applying and hearing back.
  • Don't expect a personal reply. The City says it cannot give individual status updates. Watch Jobs Applied and your email.

Set up job alerts so you see new postings early. A posting can open and close inside two weeks.

Jobs with different hiring steps

Some City jobs have their own recruitment pages. Two of them list extra steps:

  • Recreation jobs are part-time, seasonal or year-round. They use a separate page, jobs.toronto.ca/recreation. The recreation hiring page lists a pre-screening questionnaire and a panel interview of up to about 20 minutes. Before training, you send your Vulnerable Sector Check results, a PHCD (Principles of Healthy Child Development) certificate and a First Aid certificate.
  • Corporate Security adds a written test and an in-person event with a fitness assessment. It also asks for at least three professional references, per its process page.

Fire, paramedic, long-term care, solid waste, Toronto Water and information technology jobs also have their own pages on the City's recruitment initiatives list. Read the page for your job before you apply.

City agencies hire separately

The City's agencies and corporations page says each agency is its own employer, separate from the City. That includes the TTC, Toronto Community Housing, the Toronto Parking Authority and the Toronto Zoo. Their jobs are not City jobs, and you apply on their own sites.

PublicJobs.ca lists several of them: TTC, Toronto Community Housing, Toronto Parking Authority and the Toronto Zoo.

What if you already work for the City?

Current City employees use a separate Internal Jobs page. The Payroll Program Assistant 1 posting says City employees must apply to full-time and part-time jobs on the internal portal. The Administrative Assistant 2 posting says City employees can apply, but cannot hold two different jobs. They must say they are a current employee on the form and give their employee number. Read the note for City employees on each posting.

Frequently asked questions

Will the City update me on my application?

The Help page says the City gets a high volume of applications and cannot give individual status updates. Status messages go to the email on your profile, and your status shows under Jobs Applied.

Can I apply to more than one City job?

Yes. You can apply to as many postings as you qualify for. The Help page says you can use a different resume for each one.

What time do City of Toronto postings close?

Before 11:59 p.m. Eastern time on the closing date shown in the Posting Period.

Does the City accept paper resumes?

The recreation hiring page says mailed, hand-delivered and faxed resumes are no longer accepted. For other jobs, the City's pages say to apply online through your candidate profile.

Is the TTC part of the City of Toronto?

The TTC is a City agency, but it is its own employer. It hires through its own Career Centre, not jobs.toronto.ca. Our TTC hiring process guide explains its steps.

How do I ask for accommodation?

Accommodation means a change to how you apply, test or work, for example because of a disability. You can ask at any stage, including the application. The City's hiring policies page says to email accommodationforapplication@toronto.ca with the job ID and title.

Summary

  • Make a profile on jobs.toronto.ca and set up job alerts.
  • Read the facts block on each posting: job type, pay, union, positions and closing date.
  • Write your resume to the Key Qualifications list.
  • Prepare for a panel with behavioural questions, and have your references ready.
  • Expect checks before the offer, such as references, education or a police check.
