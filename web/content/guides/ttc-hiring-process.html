The Toronto Transit Commission (TTC) is the public transit agency that runs Toronto's buses, streetcars and subway. It hires through its own online Career Centre. There are two main paths. Most jobs follow a shorter process built around a phone screen and a panel interview. Transit Operator jobs follow a longer process with tests, a driving assessment and medical checks.

This guide explains both paths using the TTC's own careers pages, its interview guide and current TTC postings. Facts were last checked on October 27, 2026.

Key takeaways

  • You apply online through the TTC Career Centre. Only people selected for an interview are contacted.
  • TTC interviews can include a 30-minute phone call and a 1-hour panel of 2 to 4 people, including the hiring manager.
  • Transit Operator hiring has 8 steps: application, online cognitive test, written road safety test, panel interview, job shadowing, in-vehicle driving test, conditional offer, then training.
  • Operators need a full Class G licence and a 3-year driver's abstract with no demerit points, no convictions and no at-fault accidents.
  • The TTC pages checked for this guide do not say how long hiring takes. The last Transit Operator posting closed on October 23, 2026.
  • PublicJobs.ca is independent and not affiliated with the TTC. You apply on the TTC's own site.

How TTC hiring works for most jobs

For most TTC jobs, you apply online, wait to be contacted, then do a phone screen and a panel interview. Some roles add a test after the panel.

You need a candidate profile on the TTC Career Centre before you can apply. The TTC jobs FAQ says all applications must be made online. It does not accept them by fax or mail. You get a confirmation on screen and by email.

Once you submit, you cannot edit your resume or answers. You can withdraw and apply again, but only before the posting closes. The FAQ prefers PDF or Word files, so your profile fills in correctly.

Only applicants chosen for an interview are contacted. If your status shows "In Progress", the TTC says to check your dashboard for updates.

The TTC interview: phone screen, then panel

The TTC's Interview Preparation Guide describes two interview stages. You may have one or both.

  1. Phone interview. A 30-minute talk with someone from the TTC's Talent Acquisition team. They check your fit for the job. You can ask about the role, the team and the hiring manager.
  2. Panel interview. A 1-hour structured interview with 2 to 4 panel members, including the hiring manager. It can be on Microsoft Teams or in person at a TTC location. Panel members take turns asking questions.

The panel may be the last step. For some roles, another assessment follows. The TTC says it will give details if that applies to you.

Panels ask four kinds of questions:

Question type What it checks Example of what to prepare
General Your work history, skills and knowledge Dates, reasons you joined or left past jobs
Technical or job-specific Whether you understand the job's tasks and procedures The duties listed in the posting
Behavioural How you used a skill in the past A real story told with STAR
Situational How you would handle a problem Your steps, in order, and why

STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action and Result. The TTC recommends it for behavioural questions. Examples can come from work or from outside work.

The guide's other tips are practical:

  • Say "I" for what you did yourself, and "we" only for team work.
  • Ask for a question to be repeated if you need to. You can also ask to come back to a hard question at the end.
  • Read the posting several times and match your stories to its main duties.
  • For a virtual interview, test your connection first.

One more rule appears in current postings. If you are selected for an interview, you must tell the TTC the name, relationship and position of any relative who works there.

The Transit Operator hiring process, step by step

Transit Operators drive TTC buses, streetcars and subway trains. The TTC lists 8 steps on its Transit Operator recruitment process page.

  1. Application and screening. Apply online. Talent Management reviews your application and contacts you by email if you are selected.
  2. Cognitive ability assessment. A mandatory online test of how well you learn, solve problems and take in training.
  3. Road safety written assessment. An in-person written test at a TTC facility. It checks your road safety knowledge.
  4. Panel interview. Talent Management staff ask questions about the skills, knowledge and abilities the job needs. The FAQ says operator interviews mix behavioural and situational questions, so prepare STAR stories.
  5. Job shadowing. A mandatory ride-along on different transit modes and busy routes, so you see the job before you take it.
  6. In-vehicle assessment. A mandatory driving test overseen by an MTO-certified instructor. It checks safe and defensive driving.
  7. Conditional offer. The offer depends on a pre-placement medical, drug testing, a Vulnerable Sector police check, and checks of your work history, education and driver's abstract.
  8. Documentation and training. About 2 weeks before you start, the TTC confirms your training date and location. Dates depend on operational needs.

Transit Operator requirements

You need a full Ontario Class G licence, a clean 3-year driving record, Grade 12 or equivalent, in-person customer service experience and some accessibility experience. The TTC lists its requirements on its Transit Operator requirements page:

  • Licence: a non-probationary Ontario Class G licence in good standing. A G2 is not enough, even if you are waiting for a road test.
  • Customer service: 1 to 2 years of in-person customer service experience, and the ability to communicate courteously with the public.
  • Accessibility: experience with disability accommodation and accessibility requirements.
  • Education: a Grade 12 Ontario Secondary School Diploma or equivalent. A 2-year full-time college diploma or an accredited university degree also counts.
  • Shifts: you must be able to work nights, split shifts, weekends and holidays.
  • Assets, not required: large commercial vehicle experience, and professional experience working with people with disabilities.

The Transit Operator FAQ adds three points. There is no minimum time you must have held your G. Training covers any licence upgrades. And the TTC's jobs FAQ says there are no age restrictions.

What the operator job is like, per the FAQ: it is permanent and full-time. Shifts run around the clock and are picked by seniority every 4 to 6 weeks. Training is like a college-level course, with written and practical tests and homework. New operators stay in their mode (bus, streetcar or subway) for 2 years before they can apply to transfer.

If you studied outside Canada, the education requirements page accepts equivalency reports only from WES, the University of Toronto's Comparative Education Service, or ICAS. Documents must be originals. Photocopies are not accepted.

The driver's abstract: stricter than you might expect

A driver's abstract is your Ontario driving record from the Ministry of Transportation. The TTC's abstract requirements ask for a 3-year abstract that shows:

  • no demerit points,
  • no infractions, offences or convictions, and
  • no at-fault accidents

within those 3 years. This is stricter than Metrolinx's rule for GO bus drivers. Our GO bus driver guide explains that Metrolinx allows up to two demerit points.

Do not send your abstract with your application. You consent to the check during hiring. Check your own record before you apply. Ontario's 3-year driver record costs $12 uncertified (a PDF by email when ordered online) or $18 certified (processed within 15 business days, then mailed). It shows your demerit point total and your convictions and suspensions from the past 3 years.

How long does TTC hiring take?

None of the TTC pages checked for this guide give an overall hiring timeline. They give only a few fixed points:

  • Posting windows can be short. A Records and Information Clerk posting went up on October 23, 2026 and closes October 30. Seasonal Janitor and Labourer postings run from October 12 to November 2, 2026.
  • Start dates can move. The operator FAQ says start dates can slip by several weeks for operational reasons. If that happens, you join a waitlist.
  • Reapplying takes a year. If you reached the assessment stage or later in operator hiring and left the process, you can reapply 12 months later.

Some postings list "For Future Vacancies" or "For Future Opportunities" where others give a number of vacancies. The TTC does not explain the phrase, but it suggests the posting may fill jobs later rather than one job now.

What TTC jobs pay

Pay depends on the job. The current TTC postings checked for this guide all showed a pay range. Transit Operator pay was not on the TTC's operator pages on October 27, 2026, and the last posting had closed, so this guide does not quote a rate.

Current postings on October 27, 2026 showed these ranges:

  • Janitor, Plant and Labourer, Buildings: $26.36 to $34.09 an hour (Wage Group 2), 40 hours a week, seasonal from about March to November 2027.
  • Records and Information Clerk: $51,487.80 to $64,337 a year, 35 hours a week, day shift.

Operators, collectors, cleaning staff and maintenance workers are represented by ATU Local 113, which says it has nearly 12,500 members.

Special Constables: a separate, longer process

TTC Special Constables have their own 9-step process. It adds a physical test, a psychological assessment, background investigations and a medical with vision and hearing standards. Read that page in full if this is the role you want.

Frequently asked questions

How long does the TTC hiring process take?

The TTC pages checked for this guide do not say. Operator hiring has 8 steps, and operator start dates can move by several weeks. For operator jobs, the TTC contacts selected candidates by email, so check it often.

Can I apply to be a TTC operator with a G2 licence?

No. The TTC requires a full Class G licence. Its FAQ says to apply once you have it.

Does the TTC contact every applicant?

No. Only applicants selected for an interview are contacted. You can check your status on your Career Centre dashboard.

Is the TTC Transit Operator job part-time?

No. The TTC says it is permanent and full-time, with no part-time hours.

Can I use education from outside Canada?

For operator jobs, the TTC accepts education from outside Canada if it is assessed by WES, the University of Toronto's Comparative Education Service, or ICAS, and you provide originals. PublicJobs.ca cannot advise on eligibility, so check each posting.

Can I reapply if I am not successful?

For operator jobs, yes. If you reached the assessment stage or later, you can reapply 12 months after leaving the process.

Summary

  • Make a TTC Career Centre profile and set up job alerts, especially for Transit Operator.
  • For most jobs, prepare for a 30-minute phone screen and a 1-hour panel. Use STAR for behavioural questions.
  • For operator jobs, check your abstract first. The TTC wants no demerit points, no convictions and no at-fault accidents in 3 years.
  • Expect 8 steps for operators, including two tests, job shadowing and a driving assessment.
