Who hires for government jobs in Toronto?

Government jobs in Toronto come from three levels of government and the organizations each one owns:

  • Federal. Government of Canada departments, such as Transport Canada, plus federal Crown corporations, such as Canada Post and CMHC.
  • Provincial. The Ontario Public Service (the province's ministries), plus provincial agencies and companies, such as Metrolinx, the LCBO and Ontario Power Generation.
  • Municipal. The City of Toronto, plus its agencies, such as the TTC, and its corporations, such as Toronto Hydro.

This matters for your search. A job at the government itself (a federal department, an Ontario ministry or a City division) is posted on that government's main careers site. A job at an organization the government owns is usually posted on that organization's own site.

Federal government: departments and Crown corporations

The federal public service is the workforce of the Government of Canada's departments and agencies. It had 345,282 employees at the end of March 2026. That is about 12,600 fewer than a year before, according to Treasury Board figures reported by BNN Bloomberg. The Treasury Board keeps the count on its population of the federal public service page. A smaller workforce can mean fewer new federal openings, so it helps to watch more than one level of government.

Federal departments with jobs in the GTA include Transport Canada and the Department of Justice Canada. CSIS, Canada's security intelligence service, hires separately and requires a high-level security clearance. See our guide to how CSIS hiring works.

A Crown corporation is a company owned by the government that runs like a business. The Treasury Board describes Crown corporations as "government organizations that operate following a private sector model" (Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat). They are "directly owned by the Government of Canada". The Treasury Board's list of Crown corporations had 44 entries when checked on November 18, 2026.

Ontario government: the Ontario Public Service and provincial agencies

The Ontario Public Service (OPS) is the workforce of the Government of Ontario's ministries. Ontario's About the Ontario Public Service page says it has more than 60,000 public servants. Toronto is the provincial capital, so many OPS jobs are based here. On November 18, 2026, PublicJobs.ca listed 34 open OPS jobs.

Ontario also owns many agencies and companies. They are separate from the ministries, and many hire their own staff. Three large examples:

  • Metrolinx runs GO Transit and UP Express. The Metrolinx Act calls it a Crown agency. In plain terms, it is owned by and answers to the Ontario government. It had 50 open jobs on PublicJobs.ca on November 18, 2026.
  • The LCBO sells alcohol across Ontario. Its corporate governance page calls it "a provincial government enterprise reporting to the Minister of Finance".
  • Ontario Power Generation (OPG) produces electricity. Ontario's Public Appointments Secretariat describes OPG as a commercial company owned by the Province of Ontario.

Other provincial organizations with GTA jobs include the Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation (OLG), Infrastructure Ontario and the Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB). Ontario publishes a list of provincial agencies as open data.

For applying to a ministry job, see our guide to how the Ontario Public Service hires.

City of Toronto: the City, its agencies and its corporations

The City of Toronto had 44,335 active employees at the end of September 2026, according to its workforce statistics. That count does not include the City's agencies and corporations, which employ their own staff.

The City's agencies and corporations chart (March 2025) splits them into two main groups:

  • Service agencies. These include the Toronto Transit Commission (TTC), the Toronto Public Library Board, the Toronto Zoo, Exhibition Place and CreateTO.
  • City corporations. These include Toronto Hydro, Toronto Community Housing, Toronto Seniors Housing and Casa Loma Corporation.

The City says its agencies and corporations "generally operate at arm's length or independently of the City" (City of Toronto). That means they are run separately from the City. For job seekers, it usually means separate careers sites. A TTC job is not posted on the City's job site.

Open jobs on PublicJobs.ca on November 18, 2026: 13 at the City of Toronto, 34 at the TTC, 15 at Toronto Hydro and 7 at Toronto Community Housing.

The GTA's other cities and regions, such as Mississauga, Brampton and Halton Region, are separate employers with their own careers sites. For one example, see our guide to getting a job at the City of Burlington.

Step 1: Choose your level of government and your employers

Pick two or three employers that fit your skills, rather than applying everywhere. A short list makes it easier to watch for new jobs and to tailor each application.

Some questions to sort your options:

  • What kind of work do you do? Transit and trades skills fit the TTC, Metrolinx and OPG. Policy and program work fits the OPS and federal departments. Finance and investment work fits Crown corporations such as CPPIB, BDC and EDC.
  • Do you speak French? Some federal jobs ask for both English and French. The job ad states the language requirement.
  • How much time do you have? Federal hiring can take many months (see below).

Step 3: Read the job ad and check you are eligible

The job ad tells you who can apply, what you will be measured against, and when it closes. Read it before you write anything.

Look for these parts:

  • Who can apply. Some jobs are open to everyone. Others are only for current employees. Ontario's job ads, for example, say whether a job is open or restricted to current OPS employees.
  • Work eligibility. Ontario's hiring process page sets the rule for OPS jobs. You must be a Canadian citizen, a permanent resident, or authorized in writing to work in Canada. Rules differ by employer, so check each ad. PublicJobs.ca cannot give immigration or eligibility advice.
  • The qualifications. The City of Toronto calls them Key Qualifications. Federal ads list essential qualifications (required) and asset qualifications (nice to have, not required). This list is what the employer scores you against.
  • The closing date and time. Both the City of Toronto and the OPS close applications at 11:59 p.m. Eastern time on the closing date, per the City's Help page and Ontario's hiring process page. Late applications are not accepted.
  • The pay and union. Many government ads show a pay range and say whether the job is unionized. City of Toronto ads, for example, list the union, such as CUPE Local 79. Local 79 is the City's largest union, per its workforce statistics.

Step 4: Write an application that matches the qualifications

Government employers screen applications against the qualifications in the ad. The best application shows, one by one, how you meet each qualification, with a real example.

Each level gives its own advice:

  • City of Toronto. The City's job application tips say to tailor a separate resume for each job. Keep it to 2 or 3 pages and show what you achieved. Leave out personal details such as your date of birth.
  • Ontario Public Service. Your cover letter and resume together should be no longer than 5 pages. Send them as one file, with the Job ID from the ad. See our OPS hiring guide for the full instructions and Ontario's sources.
  • Federal government. The Public Service Commission's how to apply page says to apply only if you have all the essential qualifications. It also warns that GC Jobs does not save your work automatically, so write long answers in a separate document first.

Step 5: Prepare for tests and a panel interview

If your application moves to the next step, expect a test, a panel interview, or both. A panel interview has more than one interviewer, and each candidate gets the same questions.

Ontario says its panels usually have 2 to 4 evaluators, and each answer is scored against an expected answer. The City of Toronto says to expect behavioural questions. A behavioural question asks how you handled something in the past. For example (ours, not the City's): "Tell us about a time you had to meet a tight deadline."

To prepare:

  • Go through the qualifications in the job ad. For each one, have a short, real story ready: the situation, what you did, and the result.
  • Read about the employer's services and plans on its own website.
  • Ask for any accommodation you need, such as a change to how a test is given, as soon as you are invited. The Public Service Commission's what to expect page says to tell the person who invites you as soon as possible.

Step 6: Pass reference, security and other checks

Before a final offer, government employers check your references and often your background. The checks depend on the job.

  • City of Toronto. The City may check references, your driver's licence, your health, your education and your police record, depending on the role.
  • Ontario Public Service. Some job ads list a security screening check. Then you need a clearance before you start.
  • Federal government. The Public Service Commission lists references and security screening as one of the steps in its hiring process.

How long does it take to get a government job in Toronto?

It depends on the employer and the job. Federal hiring has the longest published timeline. The Public Service Commission says each step in the process can take 4 weeks to a few months. It also reports that the median time to hire a new federal candidate was 227 days in 2021.

Ontario keeps each job ad open for at least 10 working days, per its hiring process page. The City of Toronto does not publish a timeline. On November 18, 2026, 676 jobs on PublicJobs.ca (about 1 in 5) were set to close within 7 days, so check closing dates often. You cannot apply after the closing time.

Frequently asked questions

Is the TTC a government job?

Yes. The TTC is a service agency of the City of Toronto, per the City's agencies and corporations chart. It hires its own staff on its own careers site, not on the City's job site. See our TTC hiring process guide.

What is the difference between a government job and a Crown corporation job?

A government job is in a department, ministry or City division. A Crown corporation or agency job is at a separate organization that the government owns, such as Canada Post or Metrolinx. Crown corporations and agencies have their own management and careers sites, and often their own pay scales.

Where can I find all government jobs in Toronto in one place?

No single government site lists jobs from all three levels. Each employer posts on its own site. PublicJobs.ca, an independent job board, collects current openings from many GTA public employers every weekday and links each job to the employer's own posting. It does not cover every employer. For example, Toronto Public Library and school boards are not included, so check each employer's own site too.

Do I need to be a Canadian citizen to work for the government in Toronto?

It depends on the employer and the job. For Ontario Public Service jobs, you must be a citizen, a permanent resident, or authorized in writing to work in Canada. Check each job ad's requirements. PublicJobs.ca cannot give immigration or eligibility advice.

Are government employers in Toronto hiring right now?

Yes. On November 18, 2026, PublicJobs.ca listed 3,483 open jobs at 155 public employers across the GTA, including hospitals, universities and colleges. Of the government employers in this guide, Metrolinx had the most (50), followed by the Ontario Public Service (34) and the TTC (34). The job stats page has the latest counts.

Summary

  • Decide on federal, provincial or municipal work, then pick two or three employers.
  • Remember that agencies and Crown corporations, such as the TTC, Metrolinx and Canada Post, hire on their own sites.
  • Write to the qualifications in each job ad, with a real example for each.
  • Prepare stories for a panel interview, and expect reference and security checks.
  • Apply early, and allow months for federal hiring.
