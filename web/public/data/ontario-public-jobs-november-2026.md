# Ontario Public-Sector Jobs Snapshot, November 2026

**Source: PublicJobs.ca (https://publicjobs.ca), data as of November 19, 2026**
Suggested citation: PublicJobs.ca. (2026). Ontario Public-Sector Jobs Snapshot, November 2026 (data as of November 19, 2026). https://publicjobs.ca/data/ontario-public-jobs-november-2026

## Key findings (as of November 19, 2026)

- **3,497 open public-sector jobs** from **155 employers** are currently tracked by PublicJobs.ca, mostly in the Greater Toronto Area.
- **65.0%** of postings (2,274) show a salary that can be read unambiguously.
- Of those, **53.4%** (1,214 postings) have a pay range that reaches **$100,000 or more**, and **16.4%** (374) start at $100,000 or more.
- The **median posted salary is $90,750** a year (range midpoint, full-time-equivalent). Excluding hospitals it is $92,693.
- **Hospitals account for 59.5%** of tracked openings (2,079 jobs), followed by universities and colleges (13.8%).
- **729 postings close within 7 days** (Nov 19-26). Of the postings that state both dates, the average posting window is 33.5 days (median 15).
- **72.8%** of postings are in Toronto; York, Peel, Durham and Halton together account for 24.6%.

## Methodology and caveats

- Scope: open postings in PublicJobs.ca's database as of the November 19, 2026 refresh. 3,549 postings were in the feed; 52 whose posted closing date was before Nov 19, 2026 were excluded, leaving 3,497 current openings (postings with no closing date are kept).
- Coverage: PublicJobs.ca tracks 155 employers with open roles, concentrated in the Greater Toronto Area (hospitals, universities and colleges, municipalities, Ontario agencies, federal bodies with GTA postings). It is not a census of every Ontario public-sector job; hospitals are 59.5% of tracked postings, so overall figures lean toward hospital pay and hiring.
- Source of postings: employer career sites and official government job boards, collected by PublicJobs.ca. Each posting is counted once, as listed by the employer.
- Salary normalization: hourly pay x 1,950 hours per year (37.5 h/week x 52, the full-time-equivalent basis PublicJobs.ca uses); weekly pay x 52; annual pay used as posted. Annualized hourly figures are full-time-equivalent, not actual earnings (many hospital roles are part-time). Using 2,080 hours instead raises the overall median midpoint from $90,750 to $95,378.
- Salary parsing: a posting counts as 'with posted salary' only when its salary text is one clean range or amount that can be read unambiguously. 188 postings whose pay text was ambiguous (step grids, 'starting at', 'exceeds', commission, multiple pay bands) were excluded from every salary statistic. Amounts with no unit were read as hourly if under $500 and annual if $10,000 or more.
- Medians use the midpoint of each posted range (single amounts are used as-is). '$100K+' is reported two ways: range tops out at $100,000 or more, and range starts at $100,000 or more. Medians by sector and employer are shown only where at least 10 postings have a parsed salary.
- Cross-check: PublicJobs.ca's own database-parsed salaries give a median midpoint of $90,987 and 54.3% with a top of range of $100K+, within about 1 point of these figures.
- Sectors: assigned from PublicJobs.ca's employer categories; Metrolinx and the TTC are shown as Transit; Ontario Power Generation, IESO, Toronto Hydro, Hydro One and local utilities are shown as Utilities & energy; the City of Toronto and its agencies are grouped separately from other municipalities.
- Regions: the posting's location text was cleaned and matched to municipality names (Toronto incl. Scarborough/Etobicoke/North York; Peel; York; Durham; Halton). Postings listing locations in more than one region, or with no usable location ('Various Locations'), are shown in their own rows.
- Employment type and work mode are as stated by the employer. 20.0% of postings state no employment type and 82.5% state no work mode, so those splits describe only postings that say.
- Closing dates: only 48.8% of postings state a closing date. 'Closing within 7 days' covers Nov 19-26, 2026. Average posting window = closing date minus posted date, for the 1,185 postings with both dates and a window of 0-365 days.
- Not measured: PublicJobs.ca did not measure overlap with LinkedIn or other job boards, and no claim about it is made here.

## Questions and answers

Answers to common questions about Ontario and GTA public-sector jobs, using only the figures in this snapshot (data as of November 19, 2026).

### How do I get a government job in Ontario?

Find an open posting, then apply on the employer's own careers site; each public-sector employer runs its own hiring. PublicJobs.ca lists the postings and links to each employer's application page. As of November 19, 2026 it tracked 3,497 open jobs from 155 employers. Step-by-step hiring guides exist for the City of Toronto (https://publicjobs.ca/guides/how-city-of-toronto-hiring-works/), GO Transit bus drivers (https://publicjobs.ca/guides/how-to-become-a-go-bus-driver/), CSIS (https://publicjobs.ca/guides/how-csis-hiring-works/) and the City of Burlington (https://publicjobs.ca/guides/how-to-get-a-job-at-the-city-of-burlington/).

### How many government jobs are open in Ontario right now?

As of November 19, 2026, PublicJobs.ca tracked 3,497 open public-sector jobs from 155 employers, concentrated in the Greater Toronto Area (GTA). By sector: hospitals 2,079 (59.5%), universities and colleges 484, Ontario agencies, boards and commissions 228, municipal governments 214, federal Crown corporations 109, transit 87, the federal public service (GC Jobs) 70 and the Ontario Public Service 37. This is the set of employers PublicJobs.ca tracks, not every public-sector job in Ontario.

### How much do government jobs pay in Ontario?

The median posted salary across tracked public-sector jobs is $90,750 a year (midpoint of each posted range, hourly pay annualized at 1,950 hours). Excluding hospitals it is $92,693. By range midpoint, 33.6% of jobs with a posted salary are under $75,000, 40.1% are $75,000 to $99,999 and 26.3% are $100,000 or more. Sector medians range from $72,984 (municipal governments) to $111,993 (Utilities & energy). Based on 2,274 postings with a readable salary; posted ranges, not actual earnings.

### What are the highest-paying government jobs in Ontario?

The highest posted pay among tracked jobs is a $500,000 to $600,000 Associate/Full Professor - General Surgery role at University of Toronto. All of the top 10 postings are physician or academic clinician roles (surgeons, radiologists and similar) at the University of Toronto, SickKids and Sunnybrook, with ranges topping out between $400,000 and $600,000. Outside that group, the sectors with the highest median posted salary are Utilities & energy ($111,993), City of Toronto & its agencies/corporations ($111,108) and Transit ($110,641). The University of Toronto has the highest employer median among the top 15 employers ($141,731).

### What percentage of Ontario public-sector jobs pay $100,000 or more?

Of the 2,274 tracked jobs with a readable posted salary, 53.4% (1,214) have a pay range that reaches $100,000 or more, and 16.4% (374) start at $100,000 or more. By sector, the share with a range reaching $100,000 is 93.7% in transit, 85.3% in utilities and energy, 65.6% in the federal public service, 27.3% in municipal governments and 10.5% in children's aid and family services. This counts posted pay ranges on open jobs; it is not the Ontario Sunshine List, which reports what people were actually paid.

### Which Ontario public-sector employers are hiring the most?

The employers with the most open jobs are Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre (448), Humber River Health (287), University of Toronto (213), Michael Garron Hospital (195) and University Health Network (UHN) (177). Hospitals are 2,079 of 3,497 tracked jobs (59.5%), followed by universities and colleges (13.8%). The top 15 employers are listed in the top_employers table.

### Are there part-time government jobs in Ontario?

Yes. 858 tracked postings (24.5%) are part-time: 628 permanent or with no term stated and 230 temporary or contract. Another 167 (4.8%) are casual or on-call, and 1,517 (43.4%) are full-time. 20.0% of postings do not state an employment type.

### Can you work from home or remotely in an Ontario government job?

Some jobs are hybrid, but few are fully remote. Among tracked postings, 254 (7.3%) are labelled hybrid, 8 (0.2%) remote and 350 (10.0%) on-site. 82.5% do not state a work mode, so check the posting.

### Where are most government and public-sector jobs in the GTA?

Toronto has the most: 2,545 tracked jobs (72.8%). York Region has 242 (6.9%), Peel Region 230 (6.6%), Durham Region 217 (6.2%) and Halton Region 170 (4.9%). The rest list several locations or none.

### Are there federal government jobs in Toronto and Ontario?

Yes. Tracked postings include 70 federal public service jobs from GC Jobs across 19 departments and agencies (most: Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC) 19, Transport Canada 12), with a median posted salary of $98,382. There are also 109 jobs at 11 federal Crown corporations and agencies, led by Canada Pension Plan Investment Board (CPPIB) (30), Canadian Broadcasting Corporation (CBC / Radio-Canada) (27) and Canada Post Corporation (17). Canada Post is counted among the Crown corporations.

### How much do TTC and GO Transit jobs pay?

Among open postings that show pay, the TTC has 15 of 36 with a readable salary, a median of $102,502 and 86.7% with a range reaching $100,000. Metrolinx (GO Transit) has 48 of 51, a median of $110,641 and 95.8% reaching $100,000. Hourly pay is annualized at 1,950 hours; samples are small, so treat these as a snapshot, not a pay scale. For GO bus driver hiring, see https://publicjobs.ca/guides/how-to-become-a-go-bus-driver/.

### How much do hospital jobs in Toronto and the GTA pay?

PublicJobs.ca tracks 2,079 open jobs at 25 hospitals and health centres, mostly in the GTA. 75.1% show a readable salary; the median is $89,417 a year (hourly pay annualized at 1,950 hours, so part-time roles show full-time-equivalent pay) and 51.8% have a range reaching $100,000. Physician roles raise the top end.

### How much do Ontario Public Service and City of Toronto jobs pay?

The Ontario Public Service has 37 open postings, 35 with a readable salary, a median of $91,879 and 57.1% reaching $100,000. The City of Toronto itself has 11 open postings and 7 show a salary, too few for a reliable median. Together the City and its agencies and corporations have 26 postings, 12 with a salary, a median of $111,108. The TTC and Toronto Hydro are counted separately. For how City of Toronto hiring works, see https://publicjobs.ca/guides/how-city-of-toronto-hiring-works/.

### Do government job postings list the salary?

Mostly. 2,462 of 3,497 tracked postings (70.4%) include some pay information, and 2,274 (65.0%) show a salary clear enough to read as a range or amount. It varies by sector: 94.6% for the Ontario Public Service, 91.4% for the federal public service, 75.1% for hospitals, 38.6% for universities and colleges and 7.3% for federal Crown corporations.

### How long do government job postings stay open, and how many close this week?

Only 48.8% of tracked postings state a closing date. For the 1,185 that state both a posted date and a closing date, the average posting window is 33.5 days and the median is 15 days. As of November 19, 2026, 729 postings close within the next 7 days (November 19 to November 26, 2026).

### How long does it take to hear back or get hired for a government job?

It depends on the employer, and most do not publish a timeline. The City of Toronto publishes none; its recreation hiring page says it could be weeks between applying and hearing back (https://publicjobs.ca/guides/how-city-of-toronto-hiring-works/). Metrolinx does not publish a total hiring time for GO bus drivers; once hired, in-house training takes 4 or 7.5 weeks (https://publicjobs.ca/guides/how-to-become-a-go-bus-driver/). CSIS takes about 6 to 18 months after the first interview (https://publicjobs.ca/guides/how-csis-hiring-works/). The posting data in this snapshot does not measure hiring time.
