Practicum Student

Location: Toronto-661 University

Department: Scientist, Third Party Grants

This posting is to fill a current vacancy.

The Role:

The successful applicant will have the opportunity to contribute to two alcohol labelling research studies led by Dr. Erin Hobin. High-quality experimental evidence testing the effectiveness of alcohol labels on consumer outcomes is limited. To address this gap, Dr. Hobin is using novel research methods permitting experimental control in the field to estimate the impacts of alcohol warning labels on consumer knowledge and perceptions of alcohol health risks, and on alcohol use. This research responds to the policy priorities and evidence needs of Canadian and international decision-making bodies, including Health Canada and the World Health Organization. The successful applicant will have the opportunity to contribute to the research processes for two alcohol labelling studies.

The first study is a randomized field experiment to test the effects of different alcohol warning labels on consumer alcohol cognitions and on alcohol use. More than 1500 participants will be recruited from across the 10 provinces, randomized to 1 of 4 alcohol label conditions, and couriered a supply of labels according to their assigned label condition. Participants will be instructed to affix 1 label to all alcohol containers in their home over a 4-week period. Outcomes will be assessed at baseline and at 4 follow-up points using a brief online survey. Self-reported alcohol use will be corroborated using lipid biomarkers. The successful applicant will assist with coordinating the initial study administration planning, materials, and applications.

The second study is a pilot project assessing a standardized protocol for the self-collection of two dried blood spot samples from participants and testing the between-subject variability and inter-day precision of PEth levels as a biomarker of medium-term alcohol exposures as compared to self-reports of alcohol beverage consumption acquired by a series of weekly on-line surveys. Two sets of dried blood spots will be collected by participants over a 4 week period using a commercial dried blood spot collection device.
