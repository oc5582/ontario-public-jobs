What can I expect in this role?

In this role, you will be responsible for receiving, monitoring and assessing incoming emergency calls, and taking or expediting the required action in accordance with Service procedures.

This is a dynamic work environment. As a Communications Operator, you will:

Answer 911 calls from citizens in life-threatening and emergency situations.

Answer telephone calls on the non-emergency, dedicated and internal police lines and take the appropriate action.

Provide instructions and support to callers in distress, building rapport and valuing lived experiences.

Dispatch police officers and support units to emergency situations; coordinating with partner agencies such as Ambulance and Fire as required.

Monitor events and adjust priorities in real time based on updates and new information.

Monitor police pursuits and gather information to assist the supervisor and the police officer(s) involved. Liaise with other divisions or other police services to provide an appropriate response

Operate various computerized communications consoles and associated equipment to receive, assess and relay information to police officers in the field. Report major or unusual occurrences to supervisor

Operate Services databases (including C.P.I.C.) to check for and relay requested information to field personnel

Perform typical duties inherent to the position such as conducting follow-up calls to citizens, other police units or related agencies as required.

Conditions of Employment:

Must pass medical assessment.

Must successfully pass the intensive training program:

Communications Operators must successfully complete a lengthy, intensive (paid) training program - comprised of 7 weeks of in-class call-taker training, followed by live environment coaching for 240 hours, and 5 weeks of in-class dispatch training, followed by live environment coaching for 240 hours. Training will include days, afternoons, midnights and weekends. You will be reporting to various locations during your training program, which will be provided to you on the first day of class. A minimum standard of 75% is required on tests and practical examinations to remain in the training program.

Communication Operators are assigned to a platoon and are required to regularly work weekends as part of their rotational schedule. All Communications Operators are also required to work statutory holidays as the rotational platoon schedule. Communication Services is staffed 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days year.

Communication Operators will handle emergency 9-1-1 and non-emergency calls.

The most serious emergencies include difficult and stressful situations such as violent crimes in progress, suicidal callers, fatalities, and traumatic events.

These calls are expected and regular daily occurrences.

Communication Operators are expected to be calm and make decisions methodically based on their critical thinking skills, TPS protocol and training.

Communication Operators will be exposed to difficult situations and require intense concentration for extended periods of time.

Due to the nature of the work, Communication Operators are restricted to their workstation, breaks and lunches are pre-scheduled and dependent upon workload.
