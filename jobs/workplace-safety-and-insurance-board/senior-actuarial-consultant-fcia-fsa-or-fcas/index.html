About the Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB)

We’re here to help. When an injury or illness happens on the job, we move quickly to provide wage-loss benefits, medical coverage and support to help people get back to work. Funded by businesses, we also provide no-fault collective liability insurance and access to industry-specific health and safety information. We are one of the largest insurance organizations in North America covering over five million people in more than 300,000 workplaces across Ontario. For more information, visit

wsib.ca

.

At the WSIB, you’ll have the opportunity to:

explore many career paths and follow your passion

continuously learn and grow professionally

be recognized for the great work you do

participate in programs that support your health and wellbeing

You’ll also receive a competitive salary and may be eligible to participate in our health and dental plan.

This role requires the successful applicant to commit to an in-office work arrangement up to 5 days a week beginning in 2027.

Salary Grade: N10 From: $132,000.00 To: $165,000.00

Help Shape the Financial Future of Ontario's Workers' Compensation System

At WSIB, your work has a purpose beyond the numbers. As a Senior Actuarial Consultant, you'll play a key role in ensuring the long-term financial sustainability of Ontario's workers' compensation system that supports millions of workers and employers across the province. Working closely with the Chief Actuary, CFO, and senior finance leaders, you'll provide strategic actuarial advice, lead complex valuation and forecasting initiatives, and contribute to high-profile modernization projects that help shape the future of WSIB.

This is an opportunity for an experienced actuary to combine advanced technical expertise, project leadership, and executive-level influence while working on unique actuarial challenges that extend beyond traditional insurance environments.

What You'll Do:

Lead Complex Actuarial Analysis and Projects by:

Reviewing actuarial methods, models, and assumptions

Providing guidance and technical expertise to actuarial staff

Analyzing and validating actuarial results for accuracy and reasonableness

Leading project teams and ensuring key deliverables and timelines are achieved

Key areas of responsibility include:

Valuation of benefit liabilities

Funding and financial forecasting

Premium and administration rate setting

Experience rating programs

Development and enhancement of actuarial methodologies and models

Deliver Strategic Advice and Actuarial Insights:

Prepare reports, recommendations, and actuarial analyses for senior leadership

Support decision-making by developing reliable actuarial models and forecasts

Monitor changes in actuarial standards, industry practices, and WSIB policies

Present actuarial findings and recommendations to internal and external stakeholders, including auditors, employers, and actuarial professionals

Lead Experience Studies and Special Projects such as:

Mortality, termination, health care, and occupational disease studies

Economic and administration cost studies

Legislative and policy costing analyses

Stochastic modelling and model enhancements

Funding and asset-liability management initiatives

New methodologies, systems, and actuarial processes

Emerging accounting and reporting requirements

Build Partnerships Across the Organization

Collaborate with stakeholders across the organization and develop relationships with external partners, including auditors, actuarial consultants, employers, government agencies, industry organizations, and workers' compensation boards across Canada.

What We're Looking For:

Education & Professional Designation

University degree in Actuarial Science, Finance, Mathematics, Statistics, or a related field

Fellowship designation (FCIA, FSA, or FCAS)

Experience

10+ years of actuarial experience within insurance, pensions, employee benefits, workers' compensation, consulting, or a related environment

5+ years of actuarial experience post-Fellowship designation

Experience leading complex actuarial projects and cross-functional initiatives

Strong communication skills with the ability to present complex actuarial concepts to executive and non-technical audiences

Disclosing conflicts of interest

As public servants, employees at the WSIB have a responsibility to act in an ethical way at all times to create a respectful workplace and maintain public trust. Job applicants are required to disclose any circumstance that could result in a real, potential or perceived conflict of interest. A conflict of interest is any situation where your private interests may impair or be perceived to impair the decisions you make in your official capacity. This may include: political activity, directorship, other outside employment and certain personal relationships (e.g. with current WSIB employees, customers and/or stakeholders). If you have any questions about conflict of interest obligations and/or how to make a disclosure, please contact the Talent Acquisition Centre at

talentacquisitioncentre@wsib.on.ca
